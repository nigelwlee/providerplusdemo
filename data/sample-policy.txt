SECTION 1: ABOUT HARBOUR CARE SERVICES
===================================================================

Harbour Care Services Pty Ltd ("Harbour Care", "the organisation") has
provided Supported Independent Living (SIL) supports to NDIS participants
across the Inner West and Eastern Suburbs of Sydney since 2019. We currently
support 34 participants across 9 shared living homes, and employ 61 direct
support staff plus 4 team leaders and 2 area managers.

Harbour Care was previously registered under Group 0115 (SIL). This manual
has been updated ahead of our transition to Group 0138 and assessment
against the new Module 5A Practice Standards, in line with mandatory SIL
registration requirements commencing 1 July 2026.

1.1 Our Model
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Support workers are rostered across each home on a rotating basis. Team
Leaders oversee 2-3 homes each and complete a monthly site visit. Homes
are leased by Harbour Housing Solutions Pty Ltd, a related entity under
common ownership with Harbour Care Services Pty Ltd, which also manages
tenancy matters for participants living in our SIL homes.

SECTION 2: INCIDENT MANAGEMENT POLICY (v3.1, reviewed Feb 2026)
===================================================================

Harbour Care maintains a documented Incident Management Policy applicable
across all homes.

2.1 All incidents, including near misses, are recorded in the Harbour Care
Incident Register within 24 hours of occurrence by the staff member on
shift, or the next staff member on duty if the incident occurs overnight.

2.2 Reportable incidents (as defined by the NDIS Commission, including
serious injury, abuse, neglect, unauthorised restrictive practice, and
death) are escalated immediately to the on-call Area Manager, who is
responsible for lodging the report with the NDIS Commission within the
required 24-hour (or 5 business day, as applicable) timeframe.

2.3 Every incident is reviewed at the monthly Clinical & Safety Meeting.
Root cause analysis is completed for all Category 1 and 2 incidents using
the Harbour Care Incident Review Template.

2.4 Trends across homes are tracked in the quarterly Incident Trend Report,
which is presented to the Director of Operations and used to inform staff
training priorities.

2.5 All staff complete Incident Reporting training as part of induction and
annually thereafter. Compliance is tracked on the Training Matrix.

SECTION 3: FEEDBACK AND COMPLAINTS MANAGEMENT (v2.4, reviewed Jan 2026)
===================================================================

3.1 Participants, families and staff can raise a complaint via phone, email,
the Harbour Care website form, or in person with any staff member. Easy
Read and translated versions of the complaints process are provided to
each participant at intake.

3.2 All complaints are logged in the Complaints Register within 24 hours
and acknowledged with the complainant within 2 business days.

3.3 The Complaints Officer investigates and aims to resolve complaints
within 21 days, providing the complainant with a written outcome.

3.4 Participants may escalate unresolved complaints to the NDIS Commission
at any time; this right is stated explicitly in our Complaints Handbook,
given to every participant and their support network at service
commencement.

3.5 De-identified complaint themes are reviewed quarterly by the
Quality Committee and reported to the Board.

Section 4 - Risk Management
===================================================================

Harbour Care has a Risk Register maintained by the Operations team,
last updated November 2025. The register lists organisational risks such
as staffing shortages, WHS incidents, and financial risks, and is reviewed
"periodically" by management.

Risk assessed roles (support workers, team leaders) require a current NDIS
Worker Screening Check and Working with Children Check before
commencement, tracked via the HR system.

Individual participant risk assessments exist for most participants,
covering mobility and general safety. Some homes do not yet have an
updated risk assessment reflecting current residents - Team Leaders have
been asked to complete outstanding assessments "when time permits."

There is no formal schedule for reviewing the organisational Risk Register,
and it is unclear from current records how often WHS-specific risks
(e.g. manual handling, lone working at night) are reassessed.

SECTION 5: HUMAN RESOURCES / WORKFORCE
===================================================================

5.1 Recruitment
All new staff undergo reference checks, an NDIS Worker Screening Check,
and complete the NDIS Worker Orientation Module before their first shift.

5.2 Rostering
Rosters are built by each home's Team Leader based on staff availability
and historical shift patterns. Overnight shifts are frequently filled by
casual or agency staff due to ongoing recruitment challenges in the sector.

5.3 Shift Handover
Staff are expected to "hand over verbally" at the start of each shift.
There is currently no standard written handover form used consistently
across all 9 homes - three homes use a shared communication book, the
remainder rely on verbal handover only, which has been raised as a gap
by two Team Leaders in recent supervision notes.

5.4 Supervision
Team Leaders conduct informal check-ins with support workers. Formal
documented supervision occurs approximately every 6 months, though this
has lapsed for several staff during 2025 due to Team Leader vacancies in
two homes.

SECTION 6: MEDICATION SUPPORT
===================================================================

Support workers may assist participants with medication where required.
Medication is stored in a locked cabinet in each home.

Staff complete a general medication awareness module as part of induction.

Note: individual, participant-specific medication management plans are not
currently held for all participants - several homes rely on pharmacy
dosette packs with verbal instructions from family/GP rather than a
documented plan. A standardised Medication Administration Record (MAR)
template has not yet been rolled out across all homes.

===================================================================
SECTION 7: MEALTIME SUPPORT
===================================================================

Meals are planned weekly with participant input at each home. Where a
participant has a swallowing risk or dietary requirement identified by a
speech pathologist or dietitian, this is noted in their individual file
and staff are briefed verbally by the Team Leader.

Staff complete basic food safety training (Food Handler certificate)
during induction.

===================================================================
SECTION 8: PARTICIPANT MONEY AND PROPERTY
===================================================================

Where Harbour Care staff assist a participant with banking or purchases,
this requires prior written consent from the participant or their
nominee, recorded on the Money Handling Authorisation Form. All
transactions are logged in the participant's Money Diary, countersigned
by a second staff member where the amount exceeds $100.

===================================================================
SECTION 9: SAFE ENVIRONMENT
===================================================================

Each home undergoes an annual WHS inspection conducted by the Operations
team, covering fire safety equipment, general hazards, and accessibility
modifications. Inspection checklists are filed centrally.

===================================================================
SECTION 10: EMERGENCY MANAGEMENT
===================================================================

Harbour Care has a general Emergency Management Policy (v1.0, issued 2021,
not yet reviewed) that provides high-level guidance for staff in the event
of fire, medical emergency, or severe weather, applicable across the
organisation.

At this time, individual homes do not have their own site-specific
emergency or evacuation plans reflecting the mobility, communication or
support needs of current residents. Evacuation diagrams are displayed in
some, but not all, homes. Staff have not undertaken a documented
practice drill in any home in the past 12 months.

===================================================================
SECTION 11: GOVERNANCE
===================================================================

Harbour Care is governed by a Director of Operations and two Area
Managers, who report to the Board on a quarterly basis. Team Leaders
report operational matters to their Area Manager weekly.

Formal, minuted oversight visits specific to each SIL home occur monthly
via the Team Leader site visit, with an Area Manager attending
approximately once per quarter. There is not yet a standing governance
report that consolidates oversight findings across all 9 homes for
Board-level review.

===================================================================
SECTION 12: PARTICIPANT CHOICE, TENANCY AND HOUSING
===================================================================

Participants in Harbour Care SIL homes generally lease their room or unit
through Harbour Housing Solutions Pty Ltd, our related housing entity.
Support and tenancy arrangements are coordinated by the same Area Manager
for operational simplicity, and support plans are discussed at the same
meetings as tenancy matters.

House routines (meal times, visitor arrangements) are set by each home in
consultation with residents, though these are generally proposed by staff
in the first instance "to keep things running smoothly," with participant
input sought afterward.

===================================================================
SECTION 13: BEHAVIOUR SUPPORT AND RESTRICTIVE PRACTICES
===================================================================

A small number of participants across our homes have behaviour support
plans prepared by external NDIS Behaviour Support Practitioners, which are
kept on file and referred to by staff supporting those participants.

Harbour Care does not currently have an organisation-wide Restrictive
Practices Policy setting out how restrictive practices are authorised,
recorded, monitored and reduced across the organisation. Where a
behaviour support plan includes a restrictive practice, staff generally
follow the practitioner's plan directly; there is no internal process for
tracking reportable use of restrictive practices separately from the
general Incident Register.
